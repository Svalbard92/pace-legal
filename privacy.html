Pace

Privacy policy

Effective 22 October 2026 · Pace for Android, by Wiseloop

In short: everything you put into Pace — bills, cards, loans, spending, statements, files — stays on your phone. Pace's server knows your username and your plan, and nothing about your money. We do not sell data, show ads, or use analytics.

1. Who we are

Pace is made by Wiseloop, India ("we", "us"). For anything about this policy or your data, write to kpchetia@gmail.com. That address is also our grievance contact under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

2. What stays on your phone

Pace is built to work offline. These live only in Pace's private storage on your phone, protected by Android's app sandbox and your phone's encryption:

  • Your bills, subscriptions and free trials, payments, cards, loans, money lent and borrowed, trips, vehicles, warranties, goals, income, savings and daily expenses.
  • Card statements (PDFs) and anything Pace reads from them; receipts, photos and files you attach.
  • Your profile (name, photo) and settings.
  • Card details you choose to save (full number, CVV, PIN) and statement-password details: in your phone's secure keystore, never in Pace's database or a backup, and shown only after your phone's screen lock.

We cannot see, read or recover any of it. Android's own cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are turned off for Pace, so none of it is copied to Google Drive.

4. Gmail (optional)

If you choose Connect Gmail, Pace asks Google for read-only access (gmail.readonly) for two things: your credit card statements, and autopay that failed. You can connect more than one Gmail account; each is read the same way.

  • Pace searches your mailbox for statement emails from banks, downloads their PDF attachments, and reads them on your phone. It reads a statement email's text only to help open a password-protected PDF.
  • If you have bills, investments (SIP, RD), insurance premiums, loan EMIs or card bills on autopay, Pace also searches for emails saying an automatic payment failed (an auto-debit, NACH or e-mandate, standing instruction or ECS that bounced or was declined). It reads them on your phone to work out which of your autopays failed, and keeps only that match (which bill, which due date, the amount) and the email's ID so it is not read twice — not the email itself.
  • No email, attachment or anything read from them is sent to Pace's server or anyone else. Pace never sends, deletes, moves or marks mail.
  • You can stop it in Pace (Cards › Settings › Gmail › Stop reading it) or remove Pace's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Pace's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We do not use Gmail data for advertising, do not sell it, do not let humans read it, and do not use it to train AI models.

5. Your phone's permissions

  • Camera — Scan & pay reads a UPI QR code; photos you take are kept on the phone. No sound is recorded.
  • Notifications — reminders and messages from Pace. Asked for from Settings, never at first launch.
  • Biometrics / screen lock — to show a card's saved details. Pace never sees your fingerprint or face; Android checks it.
  • Internet, and starting after a restart (so reminders survive it).

6. Payments

Pace records money; it never moves it. Scan & pay hands a UPI payment link to the UPI app you choose, which handles the payment; Pace sees only whether that app says it succeeded. Paid plans, when offered, are charged by Google Play; we receive only proof of purchase, never your card or bank details.

7. Backups

A backup is a file you make and keep wherever you choose. It is encrypted with your account password; we never receive it and cannot open it. It restores only on a phone signed in to the same account, with the password it was made with.

8. Sharing

We do not sell, rent or share personal data. The only services involved are those that run Pace: our rented server, Cloudflare (network delivery), Google Firebase Cloud Messaging (message delivery) and Google Play (installs, updates and purchases). We disclose data only if Indian law requires it, and we hold very little.

9. How long we keep it

  • Your account (username, password hash, plan, push tokens): until you delete it.
  • Sign-in sessions: ended when you sign out, or after a year unused.
  • Snapshots of the account database, kept to undo administrative mistakes: the 60 most recent; an account deleted after a snapshot leaves it as newer ones replace it.
  • Failure reports: as long as they help fix statement reading. They identify no one, and we delete them when they no longer do.

10. Your rights and choices

  • Delete your account at any time: in Pace (Settings › Account › Delete account), on the web, or by email. It erases your username, plan, sessions and push tokens from our server.
  • Erase what is on your phone: uninstall Pace, or clear its data in Android's settings.
  • Access or correct what our server holds about you: Settings › Account shows all of it (your username and plan); write to us for anything else.
  • Turn failure reports off, disconnect Gmail, or turn notifications off, at any time.

11. Security

Traffic is encrypted (HTTPS). Passwords never leave the phone; the server keeps only hashes of keys and of sessions. Card details stay in the phone's secure keystore. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affected your data, we would tell you and the authorities as Indian law requires.

12. Children

Pace is for adults (18+) managing their own money. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly hold a child's account; if you believe we do, write to us and we will delete it.

13. Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and its date, and tell you in the app if the change matters. Using Pace after that means you accept the new version.
